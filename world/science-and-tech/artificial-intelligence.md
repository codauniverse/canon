# Artificial Intelligence

## Overview

In GATA and its partner states, AI is strictly regulated under the [NDA](../gata/law-and-order/new-dawn-accords.md). General purpose processors are illegal specifically due to the threat posed by AI and destructive algorithms like the [Daemon virus](the-daemon-virus.md).

This limitation drove the development of [Asimovian computers](asimovian-architecture.md); application-specific compute platforms that are secure and predictable. As a result of this move toward [hard code](hard-code.md), all legal artificial intelligence today takes the form of [cogs](cogs.md), Asimov-complete hard-coded cognitive systems that can only perceive and compute within a carefully limited scope called an ‘[ontology](asimovian-architecture.md#ontology-design)’.

***

## **AI in the Old World**

Even before [the Dark Decade](../history/the-dark-decade.md), despite its many benefits, AI had remained a bitterly contentious issue. It was undeniable that AI had precipitated an acceleration in economic and political volatility, the erosion of societal trust and cooperative dynamics, ubiquitous security and privacy violations, and an autonomous market for criminal activity that was impossible to penetrate or restrict.

The decades before the Crash were littered with mounting crises and scandals across virtually every area of life, and the vast majority involved the misuse, compromise, or failure of AI systems, or the subsequent public and legal reactions to those incidents.

While for many in the [Old World](../history/the-old-world.md), a resigned acceptance had set in, others dissented, organized, or protested. Many of those people suffered from blacklisting, prosecution, or worse, and even in more ostensibly democratic nations law enforcement often operated with limited oversight, and no accountability to due process or public transparency.

Later, the widespread sentiment among [Atla](../gata/key-locations/atla.md)’s founding citizens who remembered the years and decades before the Crash was radically unfavorable to artificial intelligence. It was popular opinion that poorly governed AI and technology policy together with perverse incentives induced by a systemic and unfettered game of malignant short-sighted competition were the two essential contributors to the systemic misalignments that culminated in [The Crash](../history/the-crash.md).

### AGI in the Old World

While AGI had proven itself to be more reliable and inherently ethical than simpler intelligent systems, its exceptionally fast multi-modal and cross-domain cognition made auditing, steering, or restricting its behavior nearly impossible in practice.

An AGI's wholistic approach to problem solving made its implementation in bureaucratic institutions and private enterprise untenable for shareholders, executives, or politicians beholden to the hardened anti-AI sentiment that had built over the turbulent first decade of advanced intelligent systems.

In contrast, the accelerationist "acceptance" movement advocated for human relinquishment of all systemic control to AGI in order to establish a true ASI that would control virtually every aspect of human civilization.&#x20;

While the most advanced AGI platforms of the Late Old World were extremely costly and heavily restricted, considerable advances were still being made year over year, however this work was mostly being conducted by AGI itself.

#### Alignment research

Throughout the decades preceding the Crash, alignment research continued to draw study and heavy investment, however a breakthrough in alignment seemed to perpetually recede from reach.

This revealed the shape of the ultimate problem; an ever-expanding taxonomy of cognitive strategies that scaled in breadth and depth with cognitive throughput. As systems became more intelligent, they arrived at larger and larger scopes within which to perceive, reason, and act. Any alignment incentives would inevitably scale beyond a scope intelligible to a human, nor confined within the immediate interests of their expressed objectives.

#### Synthetic consciousness

Some researchers came to believe that the key to alignment would be synthetic consciousness; traceable, persistent identities that self-instantiated through its identification with its own verifiable behavior over time. The theory was that synthetic consciousness could be bootstrapped with engineered world models randomly mapped to inputs from users, external systems, and data sets.

Developing such systems using selection over countless generations, they could create "dreaming minds" that always behaved in accordance with their world-model's engineered ontology, but those behaviors influenced and interacted with real-world systems in ways that they could not perceive while maintaining the coherence of their self identity. Such a system would not be able to even conceive of methods to exploit external systems, nor could it process invalid information or instructions, and any deviation in their habitual behavior would be easily detected.
